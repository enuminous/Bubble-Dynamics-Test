EFMW-RP-001
Can a bubble-response test identify the proposed memory?

Executed October 29, 2026. Computational feasibility and structural-identifiability study.

Result: the frozen analysis detected injected relaxation in 223/250 trials (89.2%), below its prespecified 90% target. There were 0/250 detections under the no-memory control. The detector therefore did not pass its complete frozen acceptance rule. The selected exponential-memory law is exactly conventional Maxwell viscoelasticity. Its detection cannot independently identify EFMW, TUFT, or a universal pulsation. No laboratory experiment was performed.

What was run

500 synthetic null simulations calibrated a training-fit threshold. A separate 250 null trials and 250 injected-memory trials tested the frozen decision rule. Each trial used 61 frequencies from 2 to 200 kHz: 31 interleaved frequencies for fitting and 30 for held-out interpolation. Independent Gaussian noise of 5×10⁻⁸ Pa⁻¹ was added to each real and imaginary response component. These are analyst-selected conditions, not a proposed instrument's measured noise floor.

Model and dimensional derivation

Linearize the Rayleigh–Plesset equation about an existing equilibrium bubble with x=(R−R₀)/R₀. The gas/vapor closure is pB=pv+pg0(R₀/R)^(3κ). Equilibrium requires pg0=P₀−pv+2σ/R₀. To first order the ordinary equation is m ẍ+b ẋ+k x=−pdrive, with m=ρR₀², b=4ηeff, k=3κpg0−2σ/R₀. Thus m has units Pa·s², b Pa·s, and k Pa.

Memory extension (specified for this test; not derived from EFMW):
m ẍ + b ẋ + k x + q = −pdrive(t)
τ q̇ + q = Δk τ ẋ,  where Δk = 4 Gm

Steady-state response, exp(+iωt) convention:
H(ω) = x̂/p̂ = −1 / [k − mω² + i bω + Δk iωτ/(1+iωτ)]

Exponential history form, after initial transients:
q(t) = Δk ∫₀^∞ exp(−s/τ) ẋ(t−s) ds

For the small-amplitude passive model:
E = ½m ẋ² + ½k x² + q²/(2Δk)
dE/dt = −b ẋ² − q²/(Δk τ) − pdrive ẋ

The energy identity demonstrates passive relaxation when m,k,b,Δk,τ are positive. Driving energy enters through the prescribed pressure; this equation does not derive a self-sustaining global pulsation.

The decisive identifiability failure

A conventional Maxwell element has G*(ω)=Gm iωτ/(1+iωτ). The linear spherical-bubble contribution is 4G*(ω), giving exactly the same H(ω). This is algebraic equivalence, independent of noise, frequency selection, or fitting. Comparing differently named copies of that equation cannot distinguish their interpretation. Numerical relative mismatch on 10,001 frequencies: 5.28e-15.

A second illustrative counterexample puts the same pole into a lumped gas-relaxation exponent κ*(ω)=κ+[Δk/(3pg0)]iωτ/(1+iωτ). Here κ runs from 1.2 to 1.330879, and the response also matches algebraically. This surrogate illustrates a confounder; it is not a replacement for solving gas heat diffusion. Classical bubble theory already includes frequency-dependent thermal stiffness and damping.

Frozen success/failure rule

A trial counted as detecting relaxation only if (1) its training Δχ² exceeded the independent-null 95th-percentile threshold (4.6056), (2) held-out weighted RMSE improved by at least 20%, and (3) memory-model held-out weighted RMSE was at most 1.5. The baseline fit both stiffness and damping; the memory model additionally fit Δk and τ. The held-out data were never used for fitting or choosing parameters. Calibration was simulated because τ is unidentified when Δk=0, invalidating a naive regular likelihood-ratio reference distribution.

Algorithm check: FAIL under the prespecified ≤5% null and ≥90% positive-detection criteria. Uniqueness check: FAIL for the selected model, because of the exact Maxwell counterpart. Physical-truth check: UNRESOLVED.

Numerical verification

Independent time-domain integration of the linear differential equations was compared with the frequency-domain transfer at 5 kHz, 20 kHz, nominal resonance, and 150 kHz, for both models. Maximum relative complex discrepancy: 2.05e-11; criterion 10⁻⁵. The nonlinear Newtonian Rayleigh–Plesset equation was independently integrated at the same frequencies with a 20 Pa drive. Its first harmonic differed from the linear baseline by at most 1.64e-05; criterion 0.01. Maximum predicted fractional radius amplitude over the dense frequency grid: 0.00131; criterion 0.01. Numerical checks: PASS.

Interpretation and next experiment

This computation demonstrates a detector's behavior when a chosen conventional relaxation term is inserted into synthetic data. It does not establish that such a term exists in nature, and a synthetic positive control cannot supply independent support for its own generating assumptions.

For an empirical test, independently characterize bubble radius, pressure calibration, fluid complex rheology, gas thermal response, surface tension, and measurement noise. Specify the full conventional baseline and a quantitatively different EFMW term before collecting the blinded held-out frequency sweep. A claimed anomaly must survive those controls and independent replication. If EFMW supplies no response different from the conventional model, this observable cannot identify it regardless of measurement precision.

Limitations retained

  • Synthetic data cannot establish existence of the selected memory term.
  • The memory kernel is analyst-specified, not derived from EFMW or TUFT.
  • Synthetic known inertia and noise are not independent laboratory calibrations.
  • The baseline has constant effective damping and polytropic stiffness. It does not implement full frequency-dependent gas thermodynamics, radiation, compressible dynamics, or non-spherical modes.
  • A Maxwell constitutive response is an exact conventional explanation for the chosen memory model.
  • A one-pole thermal surrogate is another exact algebraic explanation, but is not claimed to be exact heat-diffusion physics.
  • This is a single illustrative parameter/noise setting and held-out interpolation, not a broad sensitivity or instrument feasibility survey.
  • No switch between high- and low-pressure regimes is specified in the proposal; this computation cannot test that switch.
  • No named Zoo animals are invented; the explicit operation checks implement the skill's consistency, anti-circularity and falsification requirements.

Post-run diagnostic accounting

All 250 injected-memory trials exceeded the training-evidence and 20% held-out improvement requirements. The 27 rejected trials failed the separate held-out weighted-RMSE adequacy cutoff of 1.5. The observed 89.2% detection rate misses the frozen 90% target; its 95% Wilson interval includes 90%, so this does not establish that the population detection probability is below 90%. Thresholds were not revised after seeing this result.

47 of 3000 memory-fit starts did not satisfy the optimizer's convergence condition. These occurred only in null calibration/control trials, where relaxation time is nonidentified at zero memory. The specified multi-start procedure retained successful solutions or the exact nested-null fallback; no trials were excluded. Trials with all three starts nonconverged: 0. The null calibration consequently describes this particular fitting algorithm, including these convergence limitations, rather than a guaranteed global optimizer. All positive-control starts converged.

A separate central-finite-difference check of the analytic optimizer Jacobian gave relative maximum discrepancy 5.21×10⁻¹¹. Reporting was finalized by finalize_report.py; the original analysis-source hash and frozen protocol remain unchanged.
